\title{Three-zone piecewise-affine models of astrocytic calcium oscillations: dynamics and network simulation}
\author{Authors to be added}
% Add author affiliations and the corresponding author's email here.

\begin{abstract}
We study a continuous three-zone piecewise-affine oscillator as a
phenomenological approximation of calcium dynamics in the Li--Rinzel model.
Both systems have two state variables; the simplification concerns the
vector field and regional propagation. Three parameter sets are fitted with
fixed equilibrium crossings and a prescribed neutral cycle, and their local
boundary bifurcations, periodic responses and numerical return maps are
examined. In isolated-population NEST tests, the ratios of median Li--Rinzel
to PWA run times range from 5.87 to 6.47 with SIC output and 20.69 to 20.94
with a calcium-threshold spike recorder. These represent different output
setups rather than a controlled decomposition of SIC cost. A time-varying
input exposes an unphysical negative-calcium transient in the selected AM
model, and FM amplitudes remain imperfect. The results demonstrate both the
computational promise and the limits of the selected approximations.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:introduction}
Astrocytic calcium dynamics can display amplitude, frequency, and mixed
amplitude--frequency modulation as the input changes. The Li--Rinzel model
provides a reduced description of intracellular calcium signalling
\cite{LiRinzel1994}, and its astrocytic dynamics have been studied across these
regimes \cite{DePitta2009}.

% Explain motivation for phenomenological reduction and network simulation.
% State the research question and distinguish the present two-threshold models
% from a separate high-accuracy reproduction of the full LR bifurcation diagram.
Here we investigate continuous three-zone piecewise-affine oscillators as
phenomenological representations of selected Li--Rinzel regimes. Both models
are two-dimensional: the simplification concerns the vector field and its
regional propagation rather than a reduction in state dimension.

\section{Models and methods}
\label{sec:methods}

\subsection{Li--Rinzel reference and modulation regimes}
% Give full equations, parameters, units, initial conditions, I and K_ER.
% Define how AM, AFM and FM are assessed and state the input ranges.
% Specify equilibrium and periodic-orbit continuation and stability checks.
We use cytosolic calcium $C$, the IP$_3$ receptor gating variable $h$, and
IP$_3$ concentration $I$ in $\mu$M. With time in seconds, the reference is
\begin{align}
\dot C &= [6 m_\infty^3 n_\infty^3 h^3+0.11](2-1.185C)\notag\\
       &\quad -0.9\frac{C^2}{C^2+K_{\rm ER}^2},\\
\dot h &=0.2[Q_2-(Q_2+C)h],\notag\\
m_\infty&=\frac{I}{I+0.13},\quad n_\infty=\frac{C}{C+0.08234},\notag\\
Q_2&=1.049\frac{I+0.13}{I+0.9434}.\notag
\end{align}
We use $K_{\rm ER}=0.1$, $0.075$ and $0.05\,\mu$M for AM, AFM and FM,
respectively. Regime names refer to the reference responses over the sampled
inputs, rather than proof of modulation character in each fitted PWA field.

\subsection{Three-zone piecewise-affine oscillator}
% Write the actual system, its parameters and two thresholds. Define physical
% and mixed coordinate maps and how C is recovered. State continuity,
% admissibility, rest and equilibrium-crossing constraints and their domains.
% Derive zone equilibria and Jacobians, including trace and determinant.
% Explicitly say when only one equilibrium exists; do not assume this globally.
The fitted system uses physical $C$ and a hidden coordinate $q$:
\begin{align}
\dot C&=a[q-N(C,I)],\quad \dot q=b[H(C,I)-q],\\
N(C,I)&=\alpha_0+\alpha_1 C+\alpha_I I\notag\\
       &\quad +\alpha_L(C-C_L)_++\alpha_R(C-C_R)_+,\notag\\
H(C,I)&=\beta_0+\beta_1 C+\beta_I I\notag\\
       &\quad +\beta_L(C-C_L)_++\beta_R(C-C_R)_+.\notag
\end{align}
Here $(x)_+=\max(x,0)$, $C_L<C_R$ and $a,b>0$. This representation is continuous
across the two boundaries. In region $j$, write $N=n_j C+n_{0j}+\alpha_I I$
and $H=h_j C+h_{0j}+\beta_I I$. Its Jacobian has trace
$T_j=-a n_j-b$ and determinant $D_j=ab(n_j-h_j)$. A regional equilibrium
is an equilibrium of the full model only when it lies in that region.
The hidden variable $q$ is not identified with $h$.

For constant $I$, each regional state satisfies an affine linear equation
and can be propagated with a matrix exponential. Propagation is valid until
the first boundary crossing, which is computed separately. For an
exponentially relaxing input, augmenting the state with $I$ and a constant
coordinate yields another linear system between input events.

\subsection{Local and large-cycle mechanisms}
% Derive or cite the relevant one-boundary hypotheses and HLB1/HLB2 labels.
% Illustrative examples in Figs. 3--4 are not fitted LR models. Give the
% return-map matching problem and conditions for an orbit crossing both cuts.
% Floquet multiplier and M=1 are necessary local fold conditions, together
% with the orbit, nondegeneracy and continuation checks.
For a chosen transverse itinerary $\sigma$, a periodic orbit is a zero of
$G_\sigma(s,I)=P_\sigma(s,I)-s$, where $s$ is a coordinate on a switching
section and $P_\sigma$ is the first-return map. For continuous planar fields,
$\log M=\sum_j T_jt_j$ along a transverse orbit; $M<1$ and $M>1$ indicate
stable and unstable cycles. A neutral orbit satisfies $G=G_s=0$. An ordinary
fold further requires $G_{ss}\ne0$ and $G_I\ne0$, with transverse crossings
and a locally unchanged itinerary. These conditions are checked numerically
below; a near-grazing trajectory requires particular care.
Hopf-like bifurcations of continuous piecewise-smooth systems and the dynamics
of three-zone systems have been studied in detail
\cite{Simpson2022,SimpsonMeiss2007,LlibrePonceValls2015,ChenLiXieYue2017}.
A two-cycle bound is known for a particular three-zone Liénard system
\cite{ChenFengJiaMeng2026}; its hypotheses must be checked before applying it
to any of the present models.
For the selected models, the lower boundary separates a stable node from an
unstable focus. Reflecting the normal coordinate $x=-(C-C_L)$ and setting
$\mu=I-I_1$ gives the positive transversality coefficient
$\beta=ab(\beta_I-\alpha_I)$. The local hypotheses of Theorem 5.2 of
\cite{Simpson2022} then yield a unique stable HLB2 cycle for $I>I_1$
sufficiently close to $I_1$. This local statement does not establish a large
cycle on the full interval.

At the upper boundary set $x=C-C_R$ and $\mu=I_2-I$. The left and right
regions are respectively an unstable and a stable focus, and the same
oriented $\beta$ is positive. With $\lambda_j=T_j/2$ and
$\omega_j=\sqrt{4D_j-T_j^2}/2$, the HLB1 criticality quantity
$\alpha=\lambda_M/\omega_M+\lambda_R/\omega_R$ is 0.00998, 105.343 and
20.464 for AM, AFM and FM. Theorem 5.1 of \cite{Simpson2022} therefore gives
an unstable local cycle for $I>I_2$ sufficiently close to $I_2$. The small
AM value and near-node central foci in AFM/FM make full-precision parameters
essential. These local theorems do not prove global branch completeness.
The dimensionless illustrative phase portraits are provided in \ref{app:phase-portraits}.

\subsection{NEST implementation and benchmarks}
% Describe the actual implementation, analytic regional flow, crossing
% handling, coordinate recovery, SIC, timing boundaries and hardware/version.
% Give topologies, seeds, recording settings and model provenance.
% Distinguish network speedup from isolated astrocyte speedup.
The network simulations use NEST \cite{GewaltigDiesmann2007}; the precise
version and model implementation are reported with the benchmark protocol.
For the selected physical-coordinate candidates, we additionally time an
isolated population of 32 independent astrocytes in NEST 3.10.0, with one
thread and $0.1$ ms resolution. For each constant input and model, a warmup
precedes three timed \texttt{nest.Run} calls of 120 s biological duration.
Each call uses a separately constructed simulation; construction, preparation,
cleanup and recording are excluded from the timed interval. Model order is
alternated. A separate single-cell run records calcium every 50 ms; its first
40 s are excluded from the extrema and peak diagnostic.
The PWA solver uses regional propagation and crossing checks at NEST steps;
its event counters are enabled during timing. The NEST 3.10.0 upstream LR
source uses GSL RKF45 with absolute tolerance $10^{-3}$ and zero relative
tolerance; the exact source revision of the installed wheel remains to be
verified. The CPU model was not captured in the run manifest and must be
supplied before submission.
As an implementation check, the recorded PWA calcium was also compared with
independent SciPy DOP853 integration at the same initial state and constant
input (relative tolerance $10^{-10}$, absolute tolerance $10^{-12}$, maximum
step $0.03$ s). The maximum sampled discrepancy across the six tests was
$3.69\times10^{-8}$ $\mu$M. The LR traces were also compared at the same
samples with independent DOP853 integration (relative tolerance $10^{-11}$,
absolute tolerance $10^{-13}$, maximum step $0.03$ s), yielding a maximum
sampled discrepancy of $2.40\times10^{-12}$ $\mu$M. These finite checks do
not certify an error bound or equal accuracy for other inputs.

\subsection{Response to a shared time-dependent input}
We apply the same piecewise-constant IP$_3$ sequence to LR and all three PWA
models, with 80 s segments at $I=0.2,0.58,0.3,0.75,0.2\,\mu$M, respectively
(Fig.~\ref{fig:variable-input}). Each model starts from its own $I=0$
equilibrium; the PWA hidden variable $q$ is not the LR receptor variable $h$.
Both systems are integrated using DOP853 with relative tolerance $10^{-9}$,
absolute tolerance $10^{-11}$ and maximum step 0.1 s, restarting at each
input discontinuity. This is an evaluation input, not a fitting target, and
is not a NEST benchmark.

The comparison reveals substantial transient and phase differences. In AM,
PWA calcium becomes negative after the final decrease from 0.75 to 0.2 $\mu$M,
reaching approximately $-0.0567\,\mu$M at 321.7 s. Reducing the maximum
integration step from 0.1 to 0.005 s with tighter tolerances retains this
minimum, so it is not explained by this step-size choice. Replacing each
jump with a one-second linear ramp still yields a negative sampled AM minimum
($-0.0554\,\mu$M); in this particular protocol 5 s and 10 s ramps remain
positive, with minima after the final change of 0.0146 and $0.0776\,\mu$M.
These finite tests do not establish positivity for all smooth inputs. The abrupt input
tests states outside the sampled fixed-input fitting cycles; the model does
not guarantee calcium positivity for arbitrary time-varying inputs. FM also
shows a large first transient after the second increase. Raw time-domain RMSE
over the complete protocol is 0.120, 0.217 and $0.241\,\mu$M for AM, AFM
and FM, respectively; these values include phase drift and should not be
interpreted as cycle-shape error alone.

\subsection{Isolated astrocyte simulation time}
For these isolated populations, ratios of median LR and PWA run times range
from 5.87 to 6.47 across the six sampled conditions (Fig.~\ref{fig:random-nest}).
Both models display detected oscillations at $I=0.5$, $0.6$ and $0.7$ $\mu$M
for AM, AFM and FM, respectively; no peaks are detected after the initial 40 s
at $I=0.2$ $\mu$M. No detected peaks alone does not prove an equilibrium.
The AM quiet-state calcium differs by approximately $0.030$ $\mu$M. In the
oscillatory tests, sampled last periods are 11.50 versus 11.25 s (AM), 13.25
versus 11.90 s (AFM), and 22.65 versus 21.00 s (FM), for LR versus PWA.
These are finite observations at 50 ms recording intervals. The comparison
does not establish equal numerical error, speedup at other inputs, or a network
speedup; the trials use the same deterministic setup and are not independent
biological samples.

We also tested an event-output variant in which both implementations omit SIC
generation and emit a NEST spike when calcium crosses $0.2\,\mu$M upward
between successive NEST steps. The crossing time is quantized to the $0.1$ ms
step. All 32 nodes connect to a spike recorder, included in the timed run.
The LR event implementation is derived from the NEST 3.10 LR model; the PWA
event implementation uses the same regional propagation as above. Three
timed repeats and a warmup use the same inputs and duration as the SIC-output
experiments. The recorder and SIC-output runs therefore differ in both the
output computation and recording setup.

The median LR/PWA timing ratios for event output range from 20.69 to 20.94
over the six conditions (Fig.~\ref{fig:ca-spikes}). Recorded and emitted event
counts agreed in every run; a comparison with 50 ms diagnostic calcium
recordings found corresponding upward crossings. In the separate single-cell
diagnostics, recorded calcium matched the previous SIC run at every saved
sample. This does not isolate the individual cost of SIC evaluation versus
secondary-event delivery, nor establish network speedup or equal event
accuracy. In the AFM oscillatory test the 32 LR astrocytes emitted 288 events
and the PWA astrocytes emitted 320. Event-time differences depend on the
pairing convention, initial transient and period mismatch;
Fig.~\ref{fig:ca-spikes} displays ordinal pairs only, without claiming unique
event correspondence.

\subsection{An optional lower calcium zone}
As an exploratory safeguard, we retain the fitted coefficients and add a
continuous fourth zone below $C_g=0.025\,\mu\mathrm M$, replacing the calcium
equation by $\dot C_{4}=\dot C_{3}+k\max(0,C_g-C)$ with
$k=100\,\mathrm{s}^{-1}$ and leaving the second equation unchanged. The fields
are identical for $C\geq C_g$; the saved fitting and fold trajectories stay
above this threshold at the checked samples. This fixed gain was not fitted,
and finite repulsion does not establish nonnegative calcium for arbitrary
initial states or inputs. The three-zone global analysis does not automatically
extend to the four-zone system.

We compared the variants in the same event-output NEST implementation using
32 isolated astrocytes, one thread, a $0.1$ ms step, 120 s simulated time, one
warmup and three timed repeats with alternating order. At seven constant inputs
from $0.2$ to $0.9\,\mu\mathrm M$, the extra threshold was never crossed;
the four-zone median runtime was $1.28$--$3.89\%$ higher
(Fig.~\ref{fig:lower-guard}). In a separate reset from a shared state reached
after $I=1.2\,\mu\mathrm M$ to $I=0$, the guard was entered and exited once
per AM or FM cell. In the single-cell diagnostic, sampled every 50 ms, the
minimum calcium changed from $-0.23865$ to $0.01775\,\mu\mathrm M$ for AM
and from $-0.02223$ to $0.01304\,\mu\mathrm M$ for FM; AFM did not enter the
guard. Reset timing overheads were $2.48\%$, $3.15\%$ and $3.84\%$ for AM,
AFM and FM. These are finite tests with three repeats, not a confidence
interval or a measurement of frequent guard activations.
We also replayed the complete time-dependent protocol of Fig.~\ref{fig:variable-input} with the same initial PWA state for both variants, using independent DOP853 integration (relative tolerance $10^{-10}$, absolute tolerance $10^{-12}$, maximum step $0.03$ s). Following the $0.75\to0.2\,\mu\mathrm M$ decrease at $t=320$ s, the AM minimum changed from $-0.05674$ to $0.02241\,\mu\mathrm M$ (Fig.~\ref{fig:guard-variable}). The unguarded AM trace crossed back above zero $3.21$ s after the decrease; the guarded trace stayed positive in this simulation. Defining settling time as the first 0.1 s sample after the decrease that remains within 1\% of the $I=0.2$ equilibrium until the end at 400 s gives 13.4 s and 14.1 s, respectively. Continuous upward crossings of $C=0.2\,\mu\mathrm M$ number 8 in both AM variants over the complete protocol and zero after the final decrease. These crossings are numerical diagnostics, not tick-quantized NEST event counts.

\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}
The same three-zone field class admits all three selected parameter sets and
provides explicit regional flows, return maps and local bifurcation criteria.
The agreement is phenomenological: matching selected extrema, periods and
equilibrium crossings does not imply equivalence of the full LR phase portrait.
In particular, the FM fit has a sizeable amplitude error, and the
time-dependent-input test reveals an unphysical AM transient. The sampled
physicality constraints used in fitting do not ensure positivity for all
reachable states. The optional lower zone mitigates the tested reset responses,
but its physical invariance and dynamics require further analysis.

The isolated-cell timings depend strongly on the output and measurement setup.
The SIC and spike experiments cannot separate the costs of the logarithmic
readout, secondary-event transmission, recorder and other implementation
details. Spike trains differ between LR and PWA because their dynamics differ.
Network-level behaviour, downstream effects and time-varying NEST input remain
to be assessed before drawing conclusions about network acceleration.

\section{Conclusions}
\label{sec:conclusions}
Continuous three-zone PWA systems provide computationally accessible
approximations to selected Li--Rinzel calcium responses while permitting local
and global-cycle analysis. The measured timing advantage applies to the
specified isolated-population configurations. The observed negative-calcium
transient and FM amplitude mismatch delimit the current candidates' use and
motivate further physicality and network validation.
